Natural Disaster Safety Management is the process of preparing for, responding to, and recovering from natural disasters such as floods, earthquakes, cyclones, landslides, droughts, and wildfires. Its main goal is to protect human lives, reduce damage to property, and ensure the safety of communities.
It involves four key stages: mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery. Mitigation focuses on reducing disaster risks through safe construction and environmental protection. Preparedness includes creating emergency plans, conducting awareness programs, and maintaining early warning systems. During a disaster, emergency teams carry out rescue operations, provide medical assistance, and distribute food, water, and shelter. After the disaster, recovery efforts help rebuild infrastructure, restore essential services, and support affected people.

Modern technologies such as weather forecasting, satellite monitoring, Geographic Information Systems (GIS), and drones play an important role in predicting disasters and improving rescue operations. Public awareness, community participation, and government coordination are essential for effective disaster management.

Overall, Natural Disaster Safety Management helps reduce the impact of natural disasters, saves lives, and enables communities to recover quickly while becoming more resilient against future disasters.
